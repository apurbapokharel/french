# Curriculum — Milestone M1 (A1)

Agreed on 2026-11-16. This is the design every A1 lesson follows. The project plan is in `docs/PLAN.md`.

## 1. Principles

The course uses the methods with the strongest research support for learning a language:

- **Spaced review:** material comes back at growing intervals.
- **Recall practice:** the learner pulls words from memory instead of re-reading them.
- **Listening before speaking:** the learner hears French they can mostly understand before they have to produce it.
- **Short grammar explanations followed by practice:** a brief rule in English, used straight away.
- **Immediate feedback:** every answer is corrected on the spot.

## 2. Design decisions

- **Goals come from official can-do lists.** Every lesson goal traces to a CEFR A1 descriptor area and, where one exists, the matching Canadian NCLC benchmark. The exact descriptor wording is checked against the official documents when each lesson is written.
- **Lessons are organised by real-life situation in Canada.** Grammar is taught when the situation needs it.
- **The site is a guided core, not the whole course.** A1 takes about 60–100 hours. The site provides roughly 25 hours (lessons about 11, checkpoints about 1.5, daily flashcards and drill review about 12). Outside practice makes up the rest, and the roadmap says so plainly.
- **All four exam skills are trained,** weighted toward listening and speaking. Writing starts small, with short typed answers.
- **Formal *vous* comes first.** Informal *tu* arrives in unit 3.
- **Vocabulary mixes high-frequency French words with words useful in Canada,** at about 15 new items per lesson (about 480 across A1).
- **Every lesson has one pronunciation point,** practised in its speaking drills. These also give labelled examples for the later phoneme work (ML-5).
- **French is cross-checked.** Every sentence is checked against a trusted reference. Anything unconfirmed gets an "uncertain" flag on that item, on top of the lesson-level "unreviewed" badge.
- **Nothing is locked.** The order is recommended, but learners can jump ahead.

## 3. Course shape

- **8 units.** Each has **4 lessons of about 20 minutes** and one **10-minute checkpoint**.
- **32 lessons and 8 checkpoints** in total.

### What every lesson contains, in order

1. **Warm-up recall:** 3–5 questions on earlier lessons.
2. **Listen first:** a short dialogue set in Canada. The English stays hidden until tapped.
3. **New words and phrases,** taken from the dialogue, each with audio.
4. **One grammar point:** a short English explanation and 2–3 examples.
5. **One pronunciation point.**
6. **Practice exercises,** moving from recognising to recalling.
7. **Speaking drills:** repeat-after-me, at least 5 sentences.
8. **Can-do check:** a short quiz against the lesson's goal.

### Exercise types

| Type | What the learner does |
|---|---|
| Listen and choose | Hears French, picks the meaning |
| Multiple choice | Picks the right French or English |
| Word order | Arranges words into a sentence |
| Fill in the blank | Types the missing word |
| Dictation | Types what they hear |

- Typed answers with missing accents count as correct at A1, and the site shows the right spelling.
- On-screen buttons provide the accented letters (é, è, ê, à, ù, ç and so on).

### Checkpoints

- A mixed review of the unit plus one realistic task.
- The learner gets a can-do checklist result.
- A checkpoint never blocks the next unit.

## 4. Lesson data

Each lesson records:

- Unit, order within the unit, and title.
- The can-do goal and the CEFR area it comes from.
- The dialogue, with English for each line.
- Vocabulary and phrases: French, English, optional note, optional Canadian-variant note, optional "uncertain" flag.
- The grammar point: explanation and examples.
- The pronunciation point: the sound, an explanation and example words.
- Exercises, each with one of the five types.
- Repeat-after-me drill sentences.
- Can-do check items.
- Review status: unreviewed or reviewed, with reviewer and date.

Each checkpoint records its unit, its review exercises, its realistic task and its can-do checklist. Each unit records its outside-practice links.

## 5. Units and lessons

### Unit 1 — First contact

| # | Lesson | After this lesson you can… | Key language | Grammar | Pronunciation | CEFR area |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Hello and goodbye | greet someone, say goodbye and be polite | *Bonjour. Au revoir. Merci. S'il vous plaît. Comment allez-vous ?* | Formal and informal greetings | Silent final letters | Conversation |
| 1.2 | What's your name? | say your name and ask someone's | *Je m'appelle… Comment vous appelez-vous ? Enchanté(e).* | *je* and *vous* | The French *r* | Conversation |
| 1.3 | Spelling your name | spell your name and follow someone spelling theirs | *Ça s'écrit comment ?* The alphabet and accent names | The alphabet | Letter names (e, i, g, j) | Information exchange |
| 1.4 | I don't understand | say you don't understand and ask for help | *Je ne comprends pas. Pouvez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ? Plus lentement, s'il vous plaît. Comment dit-on … en français ?* | *ne … pas* as a fixed phrase | Rising voice for questions | Conversation |

**Checkpoint 1:** meet a new neighbour. Greet them, introduce yourself, spell your name and ask them to repeat.

### Unit 2 — About me

| # | Lesson | After this lesson you can… | Key language | Grammar | Pronunciation | CEFR area |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Where are you from? | say where you're from and where you live | *Je viens de… Je suis indien / indienne. J'habite à Toronto.* | *être*; masculine and feminine nationalities | *u* and *ou* | Information exchange |
| 2.2 | Languages and work | say what languages you speak and what your job is | *Je parle anglais et un peu français. Je suis infirmier / infirmière.* | *parler*; no article before a job | Nasal *an / en* | Information exchange |
| 2.3 | Numbers 0–20 | give and understand a phone number | *Mon numéro est le…* Numbers 0–20 | Numbers | Final letters in *six, huit, dix* | Information exchange |
| 2.4 | Numbers 21–69 and age | say your age and ask someone's | *J'ai 32 ans. Quel âge avez-vous ?* Numbers 21–69 | *avoir* | Nasal *in / un* | Information exchange |

**Checkpoint 2:** your first day at work. Fill in a simple form and introduce yourself (name, origin, city, languages, job, age, phone number).

### Unit 3 — Family, friends and coworkers

| # | Lesson | After this lesson you can… | Key language | Grammar | Pronunciation | CEFR area |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | My family | name your family members | *J'ai deux frères et une sœur. Mon mari, ma femme, mes enfants.* | *mon / ma / mes* | Nasal *on* | Describing experience |
| 3.2 | *Tu* or *vous*? | talk informally with a friend or coworker | *Tu t'appelles comment ? Ça va ? Et toi ?* | *tu* forms; when to use *tu* | Linking: *vous êtes*, *vous avez* | Conversation |
| 3.3 | Describing people | describe someone simply | *Il est grand. Elle est gentille.* | *il / elle*; adjective agreement | Final consonant heard with *-e*: *grand / grande* | Describing experience |
| 3.4 | Introducing someone | introduce another person | *Voici ma collègue, Sara. C'est mon ami. Il s'appelle…* | *son / sa / ses*; *c'est* | *é* and *è* | Conversation |

**Checkpoint 3:** show a family photo to a coworker and describe three people.

### Unit 4 — Daily life

| # | Lesson | After this lesson you can… | Key language | Grammar | Pronunciation | CEFR area |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Days, months and dates | say and understand a date | *Aujourd'hui, c'est lundi. Le 3 mars.* | Dates; *le lundi* for habits | The *eu* sound: *jeudi, deux* | Information exchange |
| 4.2 | Numbers 70–100 | understand prices, addresses and long numbers | *soixante-dix, quatre-vingts, quatre-vingt-dix, cent* | How 70–99 are built | Numbers said at normal speed | Understanding announcements |
| 4.3 | What time is it? | tell the time and ask when something happens | *Quelle heure est-il ? Il est trois heures et demie. À quelle heure ?* | Clock time, including the 24-hour clock | Linking with *heures* | Information exchange |
| 4.4 | My routine | describe your working week | *Je travaille du lundi au vendredi. Je commence à 9 heures.* | Regular *-er* verbs, all persons | Silent endings: *parle, parles, parlent* | Describing experience |

**Checkpoint 4:** understand a voicemail giving an appointment day and time, then describe your weekly schedule.

### Unit 5 — Getting around

| # | Lesson | After this lesson you can… | Key language | Grammar | Pronunciation | CEFR area |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Places in town | ask where a place is | *Où est la pharmacie ? Il y a une banque près d'ici ?* | *le / la / l' / les* and *un / une / des* | *l'* before a vowel or silent *h* | Information exchange |
| 5.2 | Directions | follow and give simple directions | *Tournez à gauche. Tout droit. C'est à côté de la banque.* | Place words with *de* | The *oi* sound | Understanding instructions |
| 5.3 | Taking transit | say how you travel and buy a ticket | *Je prends l'autobus. Je vais au travail en métro.* | *aller*; *à / au / à la / aux* | The *o / au / eau* sound | Obtaining goods and services |
| 5.4 | Where I live and go | say what city and country you live in | *J'habite à Montréal, au Canada. On va au centre-ville.* | *à / au / en* with places; *on* for "we" | The French *j*: *je, jour* | Information exchange |

**Checkpoint 5:** ask for directions to a government office, follow them, and read a simple transit sign.

### Unit 6 — Shopping and food

| # | Lesson | After this lesson you can… | Key language | Grammar | Pronunciation | CEFR area |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | At the grocery store | ask for food items | *Je cherche du pain. Avez-vous des œufs ?* | *du / de la / des* | Silent plural *-s*: listen to the article | Obtaining goods and services |
| 6.2 | How much is it? | ask a price and pay | *Ça coûte combien ? C'est trop cher. Je paie par carte.* | Questions with *combien* | *é, er, ez* sound the same | Obtaining goods and services |
| 6.3 | Ordering | order food and drink | *Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît. Je vais prendre…* | *je voudrais*; *prendre* | *qu* sounds like *k* | Obtaining goods and services |

**Checkpoint 6:** order a meal and buy three items, understanding the total.

### Unit 7 — Home

| # | Lesson | After this lesson you can… | Key language | Grammar | Pronunciation | CEFR area |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | Rooms and furniture | describe what a home has | *Il y a deux chambres. Il n'y a pas de balcon.* | *il y a / il n'y a pas de* | *ch* sounds like "sh" | Describing experience |
| 7.2 | Describing a place | describe an apartment and a neighbourhood | *C'est un grand appartement. Le quartier est calme.* | Where adjectives go | *é* and *è*, revisited | Describing experience |
| 7.3 | Renting | ask basic questions about a rental | *C'est combien par mois ? Est-ce que le chauffage est compris ?* | Questions with *est-ce que* | *est-ce que* said as one block | Obtaining goods and services |
| 7.4 | Calling the landlord | report a problem by phone | *Je vous appelle au sujet de l'appartement. Le chauffage ne fonctionne pas.* | *pouvez-vous* + verb | *-tion* sounds like "syon" | Information exchange |

**Checkpoint 7:** leave a voicemail for a landlord about an apartment listing, giving your name, number, one question and a time to call back.

### Unit 8 — Work and services

| # | Lesson | After this lesson you can… | Key language | Grammar | Pronunciation | CEFR area |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8.1 | At work | say what you can and must do | *Je dois travailler samedi. Je peux commencer lundi.* | *pouvoir* and *devoir* + verb | The *ui* sound: *suis, huit* | Goal-oriented co-operation |
| 8.2 | Making an appointment | book an appointment and say what you'll do | *Je voudrais prendre un rendez-vous. Je vais appeler demain.* | Near future: *aller* + verb | Linking, revisited: *les amis, un ami* | Obtaining goods and services |
| 8.3 | At the doctor and pharmacy | say what hurts and for how long | *J'ai mal à la tête. J'ai de la fièvre depuis deux jours.* | *avoir mal à*; *depuis* | Nasal sounds, revisited | Information exchange |
| 8.4 | What did you do? | say a few things you did yesterday | *Hier, j'ai travaillé. J'ai appelé le propriétaire.* | A first look at the past: *avoir* + *-é* | *travaille* and *travaillé* | Describing experience |

**Checkpoint 8 (end of M1):** at a bank or government office. Book an appointment, explain a simple need, give your personal details, and say what you did yesterday and what you'll do tomorrow. Ends with the full M1 can-do checklist.

## 6. Outside practice

Each unit links to free listening and reading practice at the learner's level.

| Resource | What it is | Link checked? |
|---|---|---|
| Mauril (`mauril.ca`, web version at `app.mauril.ca`) | Free tool from CBC/Radio-Canada, funded by the Government of Canada, for learning French through Canadian shows and podcasts | Yes, 2026-11-16 |
| TV5MONDE Apprendre le français (`apprendre.tv5monde.com`) | Free graded exercises built on video | No. The site refuses automated requests, so check it by hand |
| RFI Français facile (`francaisfacile.rfi.fr`) | Easy-French news and exercises | No. The site refuses automated requests, so check it by hand |

The specific page for each unit is chosen when that unit is written.

## 7. To confirm with the fluent reviewer

- Canadian usage notes, for example *comptant* for cash, *facture* versus *addition* for a restaurant bill, apartment sizes such as *un 3 et demie*, and *carte d'assurance maladie*.
- Every item carrying an "uncertain" flag.
- The exact CEFR and NCLC descriptor behind each lesson goal.
